DECEMBER 11, 12 &amp; 13, 2026 MEREDITH SUPERNATURAL AMPHITHEATRE Dijon Dijon is from the future . An iconoclast untethered in time. Slicing and dicing sounds like a spaceman who just landed on solid ground. Down under for the very first time, bringing his mercurial love songs to the One and Only Stage. A cosmic congregation, super-charged by his astounding live band. Goosebumps. Slow-jam dances. Raw folk freakouts. Cryptic r’n’b cuts that explode chests like blockbuster Top of the Pops hits. With just two records Dijon Duenas has already developed the most singular of sounds. Absolutely became the bedrock for every musician trying to do it different in the 20s. Baby took it a step further out. A collision of sound sprung from all decades, ruminations on domestic bliss stretching the bounds of rhythm and time. A classic beamed straight from the solar plexus. Many Times. Yamaha. The Dress. Baby! Another Baby! Bubbling up from his Baltimore bedroom, Dijon’s shadow-shifting production cut through the lockdown malaise. Into his orbit arrived fellow sonic sorcerers Mk.gee and Bon Iver. He helped them find their groove, they helped him find his. From there, everyone wanted a piece: Charli, Bieber, even Paul Thomas Anderson . Sonic benediction for the hopelessly devoted. Primed for heart-squeezing communal ascension. Dijon. underscores. D Train &amp; Mondo Freaks All aboard for a funnnnky Supernatural special. A sprinkling of postmeridian magic and James ‘D Train’ Williams steering The Sup’ into ecstatic freakwencies. It’s the stuff Aunty is made of. The lungs behind You’re The One For Me , the post-disco classic that shot out of the New York club scene in ‘81, wriggling its way across the Atlantic to demolish hearts and dancefloors. Alongside Hubert Eaves III, Williams lit it up as D Train, pitch-shifting the moment from one decade to the next, presiding over the birth of boogie – and stamping music for all time when his airy falsetto was lifted to forge the iconic ‘ 169 Aaaah! ’ Roland sample. (That’s to say, without Williams we may never have had Nelly’s Dilemma ). He’s sung it big on other hits too. Walk On By. Music. Something’s On Your Mind. And the grooves Keep On coming. Stoking the D Train steam engine will be Melbourne funk-o-mites and beloved Sup’ stirrers, Mondo Freaks . Saturday arvo, stopping all stations from Inner Mongolia to Outer (Outer) Space. Killing Heidi Violet Town’s very own. Teenage sensations who conquered charts &amp; hearts. Back in 1996, siblings Ella &amp; Jesse Hooper formed a band with their high school mates and headed down the Hume to play city gigs like Push Over. Ultra-talented kids, they leavened their folk roots with punk and power pop, and soon got signed to the big leagues – balancing their gritty side with radio rock sheen. A few years later, Weir hit the airwaves and caught fire, followed by Mascara – which went #1 on the day of Ella’s 17th birthday. Reflector became the fastest selling Australian album ever. KH were everywhere at the turn of the millennium, from school formals to sold out bandrooms, from So Fresh to the Big Day Out. Their down-to-earth charm oozed through, and their success inspired many to pick up a mic or instrument and start a band. Old friend of mine, we will never lose the time After two more albums and hits like I Am , in mid-2006 an announcement went up on their MySpace page that the group were taking a break. 20 years on, Killing Heidi are back, rockin’ again, alive as ever. Eddy&nbsp;Current&nbsp;Suppression&nbsp;Ring. City Of Ballarat Municipal Brass Band Those magnificent folks in their almost-flying machine. Now over 125 years old, and a popular fixture at Meredith since 2005. These civic minded alloys usually start with a march through the campgrounds to help everyone polish away the previous night’s excesses, before moving onto the stage and romping through some classic hits. They aim to get bodies moving and warm you up for what is to follow. Their origins can be traced to a band formed in Ballarat by Thomas Bulch, who had arrived from England with a group of his musician friends in 1885 at the age of 22. Bulch became a leader in the local banding scene, creating ‘Bulch’s Model Brass Band’, which was renamed The Ballarat City Brass Band in October 1900.
