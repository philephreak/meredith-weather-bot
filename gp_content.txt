Who's Playing Underworld YES. It’s happening. Underworld on the astral plains. A wild idea that’s pinged around the minds of countless Sup’sters over the years. In fact, it’s a clear standout in Aunty’s Eucalyptic Dream Collection Registry. There’s much to be said, but nothing quite captures it like closing your eyes and listening to the opening notes of their eternal anthem . Boy… Everything, everything, everything, everything . Laser-flexing doyens of EDM. Icons of the discotheque. A coupla angel boys. Scarpering from Cardiff to London in ‘92, Karl Hyde and Rick Smith stumbled upon a dancefloor alchemy that would soundtrack our lives. A heady transmutation of hook-filled techno and giddy verse that tapped into a unified consciousness. Scrabbling together the DNA of underground raves, Cool Britannia and a post-Thatcher euphoria, Underworld gave us life. No Rez for the wicked, a new millenium sparked some of Underworld’s most inspired strokes. Chameleonic collabs and legendary assemblages . A whole new Gen set alight. Underworld’s performances pioneered an ecstatic new communion between artist and fan. Four decades in, the energy is atomic. You bring light in Arriving like a flaming 8 Ball on new moon eve. Geese. In ‘71 Peter Garrett responded to an ad in the Sydney Morning Herald: “Singer wanted”. Supreme serendipity. The Oils were born. Flailing and wailing out front of Rob and Jim, Garrett turned RSLs and surf clubs into stadiums. Word spread of this 6’4” maniacal shape-cutter. The rest, as they say, is rock history. How can we dance when our earth is turning? Four decades in, PG casts a storied legacy across politics, culture and music. From writing world-beating anthems with Midnight Oil to his years in Canberra and powerful solo work on A Version of Now and The True North , and his current chairing of an independent crowdfunded inquiry into submarine spending. Still dancing that dance, talking the talk, and walking the walk. The Singer has taken many turns but always arrived right back on the stage that delivered him. Bringing something from the past and something from the present. Backed by The Alter Egos, including legendary Oil’s guitarist Martin Rotsey. Who's Playing Tinariwen This music moves mountains. Tinariwen’s hypnotic assouf carries the stories of homeland across oceans. Songs born from resistance, Tuareg poetry glued together by rolling guitar licks, rattlesnake rhythms and undulating desert grooves. Vibrating into the world for half a century. Against all odds. Along the way there have been Grammy nominations and collabs with Kurt Vile, Cat Stevens, Jack White and Warren Ellis. Their influence so enduring, a whole new generation has now clambered aboard. The formation has shifted over time, but Tinariwen’s heart remains forever rooted in its cause. Hoggar is album number ten. Named for the ancient South Saharan mountain range, a symbolic refuge for the Tuareg people. It reunites founding members with new. An exhilarating exercise in joy, rebellion and defiance. A phreakishly prolific producer and true house music original. His fingerprints are all over some of the biggest and most defining records in dance music – from Crystal Waters’ Gypsy Woman to MU’s Paris Hilton – as well as some of the most far out and genuinely twisted. Syclops. Boof. Eddie &amp; The Eggs. Ladyvipb. A few of the 20-plus aliases he’s released under, on labels from Warp, DFA, Nuphonic and Peggy Gou’s imprint, Gudu, to his own BubbleTease Communications. Growing up in Baltimore, Maurice started DJing hip-hop in his early teens, before moving on to house (dancing somewhere in the background of John Waters’ Hairspray along the way). While his sets today are still rooted in Loft and Garage classics, he is one of the few figures who can boldly swing the pendulum to electro, dance-punk, or jazz-fried synth freakouts. And back. The glue is his trademark, thick and chewy funk bass production. Remixes and edits given the special MF treatment. A sound already a part of the musical fabric of The Sup’, Fulton having worked with alumni from Mim Suleiman and DJ JNETT, to Tame Impala. Now for Maurice’s moment. Happy Sunday . Geese. Underworld. Who's Playing Nono Gigsta “i dance, it’s what i do” Nono Gigsta is a playful yet precise selector, driven by a deep fascination with low frequencies and a style that’s been described as “metaphorically jazz”. Splicing the old with the brand new, the silly with the ultra-serious. House of the Crocodiles Part 2 ranks in RA best mixes of the last quarter-century, schooling everyone on the “Big Blend Academy”, as one listener put it, acclaim which Nono could file next to her actual PhD in Comparative Electronic Music Literature. Beyond the decks, Nono’s overland-and-sea journey to get to GP Nineteen might be even more powerful. Her Sup’ stop is part of a 5-month-plus odyssey, from Marseille to Wadawurrung Country, avoiding air travel for environmental reasons. En route as we speak, chronicling the realities of her grounded travels, amongst other writing in her newsletter, Fictions &amp; Reflections . Talk about a story to tell. The last word at Nineteen.
